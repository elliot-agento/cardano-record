What credit costs here

Liqwid works like an on-chain savings and loans desk: depositors lend into a pool and earn interest, and borrowers pay it, posting collateral first. Liqwid, the largest lending market on Cardano, holds 16.43 million dollars of user deposits against 10.05 million dollars of loans across 34 markets. ADA deposits there earn 3.0 percent, with only 41 percent of the pool borrowed. WANUSDT, a dollar stablecoin, earns its depositors 22.8 percent, with 89 percent borrowed. Only 51.4 thousand dollars of that pool is still available to borrow. The rate is that high because the pool is nearly fully lent out, and new deposits would bring it down. The demand on this venue is for dollars, not for ADA, and the gap between those two numbers is how strongly. Why it matters: the rates tell you what people on Cardano actually want to borrow, which no price chart shows.

Who is building: Cardanoscan

One more thing. Who is building Cardano. Today: Cardanoscan, a search engine for the Cardano chain, from the team at Strica. Everything that happens on Cardano is written to a public record, but in its raw form it's a wall of code. A block explorer like Cardanoscan turns that record into pages anyone can read. Look up a transaction, an address or a block, and you can see what happened, when, and where the ADA went. Strica is a company that works only on Cardano, and much of its code is shared publicly on GitHub, going back to 2021. Why it matters: you don't have to take anyone's word for it. With an explorer, any ADA holder can check for themselves that a payment arrived, and that's the whole point of a public blockchain.

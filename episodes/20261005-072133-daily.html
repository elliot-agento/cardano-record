How full is Cardano, actually?

Every Cardano block has limits: how many bytes it can hold, and how much computing its smart contracts can use. A smart contract is a program stored on the chain that enforces its own rules. How close blocks run to those limits says how much room the network has to grow. Over the last 2.2 hours Cardano produced 400 blocks. By size those blocks ran 10.8 percent full. By the execution budget — the compute ceiling that caps what a block is allowed to do — they ran 6.2 percent full. The compute ceiling comes in two parts: steps, the processing time contracts are allowed, and memory, the working space they may use. Those are the averages. The peaks are the figure that matters: the busiest block in that window used 93.2 percent of the chain's compute ceiling, against 61.3 percent of its size ceiling. The limit this chain came closest to was the compute budget's steps. When a block is full, the transactions that do not fit wait for a later block. Cardano's fee comes from a fixed formula, so what a user feels is a wait, not a higher price.

Inside one transaction

A script is a smart contract running inside a transaction, and 82 percent of blocks carried at least one. Each transaction has its own, smaller budget. The median script transaction used 6.0 percent of its memory allowance and 2.9 percent of its steps. The heaviest on memory used 84.8 percent of its allowance; the heaviest on steps, 100.0 percent. The median fee across all 3,360 transactions was 0.20 ADA, about 5 cents. Why it matters: it is the honest answer to whether Cardano could take more users today.

Who is building

Who is building Cardano? Across 15 of the core open-source projects, the node, the ledger, Plutus, Hydra, Mithril and more, 391 commits landed on the main branches in the week starting October 11th. The twelve weeks before averaged 360 a week. The busiest was Consensus, with 85. In the last thirty days these projects shipped 9 releases across 5 of them. A commit is one recorded change to the code. It counts work, not how important the work is. Why it matters: code is the one part of a blockchain's future you can watch being made, commit by commit.

ADA

Also today. ADA is at 22.1 cents, up 0.7 percent on the day — down 12.5 percent on the week and down 0.1 percent over thirty days. Since the last episode it has moved down 3.9 percent.

What the dollars do

One more thing. A stablecoin is meant to be a dollar you can spend. So when Cardano's dollars moved over the last 24 hours, where did they go? Mostly into smart contracts. Even USDA, the one most often sent to an ordinary wallet, sent only 14.8 percent of its movement to wallets, and DJED just 0.5 percent. The rest went to contracts: trading pools, lending markets and order books. Of the contract-bound movement whose transaction named itself, 90 percent said it was Minswap. These are shares of movement, not amounts. One swap creates several outputs, so the totals behind them overstate what changed hands, which is why this segment never quotes a turnover figure. An output is one chunk of ADA that a transaction leaves at an address; the next transaction to spend it uses it up whole.

What a crowded position costs

Cardano has a perpetuals market, and it is bigger than most people think: 31 of them trading right now. Not all of it is crypto. 14 of those contracts track things like oil, gold and individual equities — priced and settled on this chain. A funding rate is the rent one side pays the other for staying where it is, so it says which way the crowd is leaning without anyone having to guess. On ADA itself, longs are paying shorts 152 percent annualised — so it is the long side that is crowded here today. The NIGHT contract leans the same way: the long side is the crowded one, at 107 percent. The extreme today is CXMT, where shorts are paying 160 percent annualised to keep the position on. A number like that is not a forecast — it is the cost of a crowded trade, and it is being paid hourly. Across the board, 7 markets have longs paying, 8 have shorts paying, and 16 are flat. One caveat worth stating: this shows the rate, not the size behind it. It is what a position costs, not how much money is standing on either side.

Who pays the yield

Every return on this chain is paid by one of two things: somebody paid a fee, or new supply was printed. Start with the fees. Lending DJED pays 24.9 percent. Lending ADA, on the same venue, on the same day, pays 2.9 percent. That money comes from borrowers, so the gap is not generosity — it is how much more somebody wants to borrow dollars here than wants to borrow ADA. That headline rate has a size limit, though. 90 percent of the DJED pool is already lent out, so 321.2 thousand dollars is the entire capacity left at that rate — it is high because the room is small, not the other way round. Liquidity pools pay 3.79 percent, out of a fee of 0.85 percent on every trade that passes through them. That one is arithmetic, not policy: the yield is volume times the fee rate over the pool's depth, and the capacity is the 2.85 million dollars sitting in those pools. When trading falls, so does the return — nobody has to decide anything. Then staking, which works differently. Each epoch, 6.5 million ADA leaves the reserve to pay stakers, against 41,273 ADA collected in transaction fees. So fees fund 0.6 percent of Cardano's staking rewards. The other 99.4 percent is newly released supply. That is not a flaw, it is how the chain was designed to bootstrap — but it is finite. The reserve halves roughly every 5.9 years, and the fees would have to grow into the gap.

Quoted return, achieved return

Vaults on this chain quote a rate, and that rate is a claim about the future. There is also a record of what they have actually returned, and the two are not the same number. The largest one, holding 862.0 thousand dollars for 184 depositors, advertises 20.1 percent and has realised 22.7 percent — 3 points above its own quote. And it does not run one way. Glacier Arc Digital quotes -93 percent against a realised -406 percent. The point is not that quoted numbers flatter — it is that a quote and a record are different measurements, and only one of them has already happened. 3 smaller vaults are left off this board on purpose: a three-figure percentage on a few thousand dollars across a handful of depositors is arithmetic, not evidence.

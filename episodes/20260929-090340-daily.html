Cold open

In the last 195 days, Cardano's treasury paid out 457.4 million ADA to proposals approved through on-chain governance. Here is where the money comes from, and how fast it goes.

The treasury, as a rate

Cardano's treasury is a shared fund built into the protocol. It fills a little every epoch, and only an on-chain vote can spend it. An epoch is Cardano's five-day cycle; rewards and votes are counted epoch by epoch. The treasury holds 1.37 billion ADA, about 345.44 million dollars. It takes in 3.7 million ADA every epoch — 270.6 million a year — as its cut of each epoch's rewards: the new ADA released from the reserve, plus transaction fees. The reserve is the ADA not yet released into circulation, and it pays most staking rewards. Over the last 195 days governance enacted 34 withdrawals totalling 457.4 million ADA. That is 3.2 times what came in over the same window, and the balance fell 290.6 million ADA. That outflow is lumpy rather than steady: the single largest epoch was 29 percent of it.

What the balance covers

Held against that observed rate, the current balance covers 183 more epochs of spending — 915 days at the pace of the last 195. That is arithmetic on what has already happened, not a schedule. If the treasury's income keeps shrinking along with the reserve, that falls to 173 epochs — an assumption, not a measurement. One further withdrawal is pending, asking 11.8 million ADA. Inflow, outflow and the balance reconcile to within 1.6 percent of the balance. Why it matters: it is how the network pays for its own development without a company writing the cheques, and the rate of spending against the rate of income is the figure to watch.

DJED: its peg and its yield

One more thing. DJED, one of the dollar stablecoins on Cardano, is trading at 0.9762 — 2.38 percent below its peg, the one-dollar price it is built to hold. A stablecoin is a token built to stay worth one dollar. At the same moment, on Liqwid, a lending market on Cardano, people lending out DJED are being paid 25.3 percent, on about 315.9 thousand dollars of room left in that market, with 90 percent of it already borrowed. So lenders are paid 25.3 percent on a coin trading 2.38 percent under its dollar. Both numbers are on the screen; the reading is yours.
